## What the code does

[FACT] The encoder (`marshal_bitwuzla`) supports:
- the sorts Bool and BV;
- Bool and BV constants;
- numerals, reduced modulo 2^w and passed as decimal strings;
- `AND OR XOR NEG IMPL IFF ITE`, `EQ`, `NEQ` (as `DISTINCT`);
- `BADD BSUB BNEG`, the eight comparisons, `BEXTRACT`, `BSEXT`/`BZEXT`
  (the width difference as the index; the same width gives the argument
  back), and `BCONCAT`.

Any other operator, sort or leaf throws `UnsupportedExpr`. The check runs
before the arguments are encoded, so the error names the outermost
unsupported operator met on the way down. The walk uses an explicit
stack, and every result is cached by Expr.
       → seahorn/lib/seahorn/Smt/MarshalBitwuzla.cc (branch bitwuzla-support, `959ea729`)

[FACT] The adapter (`bitwuzla_solver_impl`) implements `add`, `check` and
`reset`. A failed `add` (`UnsupportedExpr`, or any `bitwuzla::Exception`
such as a non-Boolean assertion) is logged once with `WARN`, and it makes
every `check` answer `UNKNOWN` until `reset`. The other six methods print
`Bitwuzla adapter: <method> is not supported yet` and `exit(1)`. The
constructor sets `PRODUCE_MODELS` already, for 1.5.
       → seahorn/lib/seahorn/Smt/BitwuzlaSolverImpl.cc (branch bitwuzla-support, `959ea729`)

## A bug found on the way

[OBS 2026-10-29] The first suite run hung on
`opsem/memset.symbolic.unaligned.{sat,unsat}.ll`: both processes ran for
more than 1.5 minutes at 100% CPU and over 2 GB. With `smt-z3` they finish
in well under a second. `sample` showed the time going into the
`UnsupportedExpr` constructor, inside `DefOp<...>::Print` for `ITE`,
`STORE`, `SELECT`, `BOR` and `BAND`. The old message code printed the
whole Expr with `os << *e` and only then cut it to 300 characters.

Our reading: Expr printing writes the expression out as a tree, with no
sharing, so a DAG with heavy sharing (like symbolic memset memory) prints
in time exponential in its depth. The first attempt's encoder had the
same `describe()`. It never showed the problem, because it rejected almost
nothing that opsem produces. The fix prints a small Expr in full, and
otherwise only the operator and its small arguments, for example
`bvlshr(select(...), concat(...))`. A unit test builds a 64-level
`x + x` chain (2^64 nodes as a tree) under a `bvmul` and checks the
message. Both memset tests now answer `unknown` in under half a second.

Lesson for later steps: never print a whole Expr in an error message or a
log line that runs on large formulas.
